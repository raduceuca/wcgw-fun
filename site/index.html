Act one

The aftermath

The wave already hit. Everyone shipped everything. Now there’s a lot of rubble that looks like landing pages.

The robots won. The landing pages are fine. Nobody can tell them apart.

AI made everything easy to make.It didn’t make anything easier to choose.

Every team now has infinite yes. Agents say yes to everything, instantly, at scale. Making things got cheap. Knowing what not to make didn’t.

Infinite output.An abdication of agency.Pick one person to keep it.

Not a critic of the machines. An expert with opinions. Occasional author of slop. Unapologetic killer of ideas and features.

Under the jokes: I care whether the thing is any good.

Act two

The professional no

WCGW sells the no. Credible, reasoned, monthly. Unlimited amounts of the two-letter word.

Here is a normal week. The ideas come in, every one of them a yes, and most of them meet the mallet. Whatever survives is the work.

Binned: add AI to the settings page, make the logo pop, dark mode for the invoice, a chatbot for the chatbot, can it feel more premium, pivot to agents, gamify the cancel button, ship it on Friday, a waitlist for the waitlist, one more onboarding step, confetti on every click, make the button bigger, rewrite it in a new framework, an AI summary of the summary, let the agent pick the font, ten more dashboards, make it go viral, add a sparkle icon, just one more modal, vibe code the payroll.

Kept: delete the onboarding, ship the boring fix first, ask a user, and make it faster.

I tell you what not to ship. Keep what’s worth keeping, bin the rest, rebuild the bit that matters.
